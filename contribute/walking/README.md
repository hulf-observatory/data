# Walking and bus stops: footpaths, crossings, problems and requests: community data

Sent by residents through the Hyderabad Urban Observatory's form at
https://hyderabad.urbanobservatory.in/contribute/walking/ . Updated every hour.

- `walking-reports.csv` and `walking-reports.geojson` hold the same rows.
- `walking-summary.csv` and `walking-summary.geojson` (from 18 Nov 2026): one row per place, combining its rows: the
  current answer to each question, whether people agree on it, and how many gave each answer (see below).
- Times are UTC (ISO 8601). Coordinates are WGS 84. `ward` and `zone` are the 2026 corporation wards, worked out
  from the coordinates.
- One entry is everything a person added at one place in one go (from 19 Nov 2026; `mode` `visit`): footpath sides, crossings, problems and requests (one row each, sharing a `walk_id` when there are several), plus any bus stop, which goes to the bus stop data. Rows from before 19 Nov 2026 used the first version of the questions; there were none on the live site.
- Published times (`observed_at` on the map and in the download) are rounded down to the hour, since rows carry a nickname.
- `place_type`: `gps` (the phone's location, `accuracy_m` how close) or `map` (a pin on a map). `located`: `phone` (standing there, the phone's location), `map` (standing there, but the location failed, so a pin on a map) or `memory` (not there when sending: marked on the map from memory; `observed_at` is then the time it was sent). `phone` is the most reliable.
- `item`: `spot` (a footpath: one side of a road), `crossing`, `problem` (a pin: something wrong at that point) or `request` (a pin: something that is needed there).
- Footpaths on main roads (highways, arterials and sub-arterials; OpenStreetMap trunk, primary, secondary, flyovers left out) are described a side at a time, junction to junction: `stretch` and `stretch_side` (`l` / `r` of the line's drawing direction in `roads.json`; on divided roads only the outer, footpath side). The person chose the side: `side_how` `compass` (they faced the road and pointed the phone across it; `road_deg` is that direction), `traffic` (which way the nearest traffic goes; traffic keeps left), `map` (they tapped it), `list` (they chose that side from a list of what is near them) or `stop` (the footpath at a bus stop: the stop's side). `which`: `mine` (the side they stood on), `other` (the side across the road, seen from across) or `both` (one set of answers for both sides: two rows, the second with `pair` set to the first row's id; it earns no extra points). The row's point is put on that side of the road, about 6 m from its middle line, not where the person stood. `side` is that side's compass direction (north / east / south / west). Footpaths on other streets are single points with no side.
- Footpath answers (empty = not asked because of an earlier answer, or skipped): `footpath` yes (most of the way) / parts (only in parts) / no; if no, `walk_where` road / edge (at the road edge, beside traffic) / dirt (a dirt path); if yes or parts: `height` (how high the footpath is from the road) easy (easy to step up) / high (too high to step up easily) / level (level with the road), `walkable` easy / somewhat / hard, `broken` no / places / mostly, `blocked` no / places / mostly with `blocked_by` parked (vehicles) / vendors (or shops) / works (construction or debris) / poles (poles, trees or transformers) / garbage, `wheel` (could someone in a wheelchair use it) yes / partly / no. Then, if the person added more: `shade` most / some / none, `lights` working / broken (there but not working) / dim (working but dim; since 20 Nov 2026) / none, `safe_day` and `safe_night` safe / somewhat / unsafe. Several values are separated by `;`.
- Crossing answers: `x_where` junction (at a junction where main roads meet: `junction`, and the row's point is the junction's) / between (between junctions: the person's point); `x_can` (can people cross here) yes / hard (with difficulty) / no, and if not easily `x_hard` (what makes it hard) barricade / fence (median fence or railing) / parked (vehicles) / traffic (too much); `x_marked` zebra / faded / none; `x_signal` (for people walking) working / broken / none; `x_safe` (feels safe to cross) safe / somewhat / unsafe. If the person added more: `x_divider` none / level (a level gap) / climb (you have to climb) / nogap; `x_stop` (vehicles stop for people crossing) usually / sometimes / rarely; `x_lit` (at night) yes / poor / no; `x_wheel` (could someone in a wheelchair cross) yes / partly / no.
- Problems: `problem` broken (footpath) / blocked (footpath; `p_blocked_by` as `blocked_by`) / nofoot (no footpath here) / drain (open drain or manhole) / water (waterlogging) / waste (garbage or waste) / smell (bad smell) / light (a street light there but not working) / dim (a street light working but dim; since 20 Nov 2026) / dark (no street light) / other. `confirmed`: how many people later said it is still there; `gone`: how many said it is gone. The map shows a problem until 6 months after it was last seen. Requests: `need` crossing / footpath / stop (a bus stop) / lights (street lights; no longer offered since 20 Nov 2026) / other; `metoo`: how many people later said they need it too.
- `dk` = "Don't know": every one-answer footpath and crossing question offers it (for `safe_night` it reads "Haven't been after dark"). It is kept but never counts as an answer: not for the minimum of one answer, not in profiles (counted separately as `<question>_dk` in the summary), and not in map colours.
- `prefilled` = true: the place already had answers, so the form started from its current answers and the person changed only what was different. A "still the same" check is a `confirmed` count on the latest row, not a new row.
- `lead` (since 20 Nov 2026): the row followed from another in the same entry. On a footpath: `problem` = started from the person's own problem report there (broken, blocked or no footpath), with that answer filled in; `stop` = the footpath at a bus stop they had just described. On a problem: `footpath` = the exact spot marked after footpath answers said broken or blocked. A row with `lead` problem or footpath earns no points of its own (points once). `side_how` gains `guess` (no compass: the nearest side, confirmed by the person) and `switched` (the person said they were on the other side).
- `outvoted`: how many of this row's answers differ from its road side's or junction's agreed answer (see the summary file). Only questions with an agreed answer count, and only answers recent enough to count for that question. Empty for rows not on a main road.
- `walking-summary.csv` and `walking-summary.geojson`: one row per main-road side (`key` `<stretch>:l` or `:r`, `name` the road's name, `lat`/`lng` the middle of the stretch) and per junction (`key` the junction id, `name` the main roads meeting there, `lat`/`lng` the junction), built from all their visible rows; footpaths on other streets, crossings between junctions, problems and requests are not in it. `n` (rows), `latest` (date of the newest row), `checks` ("still the same" checks). For each question: `<question>` the current answer (blank if no one answered recently, or answers are mixed; several values separated by `|`), `<question>_agreed` yes (at least 2 people answered recently and more than two-thirds gave this answer) / no (only 1 recent answer) / mixed / blank, `<question>_counts` how many people gave each answer (for example `yes:3|no:1`), `<question>_dk` how many said they don't know. Road sides have the footpath questions, junctions the crossing ones. Follow-ups count only answers where the question above matched.
- Answers stop counting after a while, by question (`keep_days`): about 3 years for what is built (`footpath`, `height`, `shade`, `x_divider`), 6 months for things that change fast (`blocked`, `blocked_by`, `lights`, `x_can`, `x_hard`, `x_signal`, `x_lit`), a year for the rest. Older answers stay in the rows file.
- Points on the leaderboard (shared with bus stops): 10 for each footpath side or crossing (5 from memory), 5 for a problem or a request, 5 more for a photo, 2 for a check ("still the same", "still there", "gone"), a "me too" or a spam flag. Rows removed as spam earn nothing.
- `note`: in the person's words. `nick`: the nickname they contribute under on the leaderboard, if any (made up; its PIN is never published).
- Not checked on the ground by us. `confirmed`: how many other people (one count per device) later said the row is
  still right; `disputed`: how many said something about it is wrong. Rows flagged as spam are left out; photos are
  not included.
- No names, phone numbers or IP addresses are collected.
